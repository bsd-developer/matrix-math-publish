## 0. Scope and normative contract

This document amends §6.5 of `0001_spec.md` by adding a second encoding for omega certificates and by requiring a Lean theorem that connects it to the existing one. It changes no mathematics: Appendix A is untouched, `AX1_combination_loss` is untouched, and the meaning of an accepted certificate is unchanged.

The words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, and MAY are normative in the sense fixed by §0 of 0001. This document is subject to the amendment procedure of §0.1 and §2.2: it takes effect only through the version increment and the ADR required by §10.

The sections of 0001 it amends are §6.5, which it replaces; §5.4, which gains one error code; §6.7, which gains fixtures; §9.2 and §9.3, which gain one recipe and one subcommand; §12.6, which gains a vector class; §15.2, which gains two manifest fields; and §16, which gains the risk rows of §12 below. Every other section of 0001 applies unchanged.

The property this document must not weaken is the one §0 of 0001 states: a published mathematical claim is backed by an exact certificate whose acceptance is connected to a Lean theorem under an explicit axiom and trusted-computing-base ledger. A second evaluation path inside the Lean checker is an enlargement of the checked surface, and §8 states what pays for it.

The prelaunch policy of §0.5 applies in full. The general encoding gains a required discriminator field, every committed certificate becomes invalid, and every affected artifact is regenerated in the same change. No default value, no absent-means-general rule, and no dual reader are permitted.

## 1. The barrier this removes

Three tree sizes are fixed by the instance and were recomputed for this document from `python/mm_opt/tree.py`:

| `ℓ*` | Nodes | Maximum-entropy blocks | Rationals in a general certificate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 91 | 6 | 451 |
| 3 | 5,779 | 762 | 32,725 |
| 4 | 1,552,339 | 207,906 | 8,882,281 |

The rational counts are derived from the schema, not measured, and they agree exactly with the committed artifacts: `tests/vectors/omega-l2-hand.json` contains 451 rationals and `tests/vectors/omega-l3-optimized.json` contains 32,725.

At `ℓ*=4` the general encoding has been produced once. That attempt reported 416,024,121 canonical bytes, 5,303 s of single-threaded production, and a 6.95 GiB peak resident set; the exact Rust evaluation of the result had not finished after three hours. Those four figures are reported by the `ℓ*=4` production attempt whose record `docs/claims.md` and `docs/experiments/omega-l3.md` both cite as `docs/experiments/omega-l4.md`. That file is absent from the repository at this document's date; §9.4 makes writing it a Stage D deliverable, and this document treats its figures as reported rather than as repository-verified. The byte figure is consistent with the derived rational count: `416,024,121 / 8,882,281 = 46.84` bytes per rational, against `1,531,818 / 32,725 = 46.81` for the `ℓ*=3` certificate, which uses the same 48-bit dyadic rounding.

What is **not** the barrier is the input path. ADR 0008 measured 10,000,368 coefficients decoded, canonicality-validated, and folded in 2.28 s at a 73.4 MiB peak, against the §6.8 limits of two hours and 8 GiB. §6.8's envelope covers decode, canonicality validation, and a no-op fold; it does not cover exact evaluation, and ADR 0008 says so directly when it records that `ℓ*=4` remains gated on the A.7 interior recursion.

So the barrier is size and the per-node exact work that size implies, not correctness and not I/O. A certificate that describes the same point in a few hundred groups instead of 1.55 million nodes removes it.

## 2. The symmetric subspace

`python/mm_opt/symmetric.py` already searches a subspace of the A.2 free variables, and every `ℓ*≥3` point this repository has certified came from it. Define:

```text
A point is symmetric when

  (i)  the free variables of a node depend only on its level and shape; and
  (ii) α_T^(r) does not depend on the region r.
```

This document names a level-shape pair a **group**. Group `(ℓ,s)` is the set of all nodes of level `ℓ` and shape `s`, which §5.2 keeps distinct by `NodePath` and which a symmetric point gives identical free variables.

Two facts about the existing module must be stated exactly, because the brief for this document rounds both off:

- The module ties `A_T` **per level**, not per group: `Parameters.logit_a` carries one six-vector for the root and one for each level at or above three, shared by every positive shape at that level. That is a strictly smaller subspace than clause (i) allows. This document defines the encoding by clause (i), so a group carries its own `A`; the current producer replicates one level vector into every group of that level. Nothing in the checker or in §5 depends on the tighter restriction, and a future optimizer that varies `A` by shape must not require a schema amendment.
- The module's docstring stated that `test_optimizer` checks that the symmetric optimum matches the unrestricted one at `ℓ*=2`. No such test existed in `python/tests/test_optimizer.py`. The tests present check that a symmetric point, expanded, agrees with the exact evaluator; that the complement pairing is the reversed split order; and that every `β` is a distribution. The docstring has since been corrected to describe what is actually checked. The value cost has now been measured at `ℓ*=2` only, and is recorded in `docs/experiments/symmetry-cost.md`: ten seeds per arm give indistinguishable medians, `7e-6` apart with the sign favouring the subspace, against a best-of-ten difference of `1.77e-4` in the unrestricted arm's favour. That is 0.57 times the noise floor of `docs/experiments/search-noise-floor.md`, so it sits at the edge of resolution, and it is 18% of the gap to the published record rather than an explanation of it. At `ℓ*=3` and `ℓ*=4` the cost remains **unmeasured**, which is what §13 records.

The value cost is also not this document's to pay. `tests/vectors/omega-l3-optimized.json` and `tests/vectors/omega-l3-uniform.json` are both symmetric under the definition above, verified by comparing the decoded free variables of every group and every region. `tests/vectors/omega-l2-optimized.json` is not: its root `α` differs across regions and twelve of its fifteen groups differ across their six copies. The symmetric encoding forfeits nothing the current `ℓ*≥3` search program is not already forfeiting, and it cannot express the published `ℓ*=2` point, which is why §7 keeps the general encoding normative.

## 3. Amendment to §6.5

### 3.1 The replacement text of §6.5

§6.5 of 0001 is replaced by:

> An omega certificate contains:
>
> - `q`, `ℓ*`, and claimed nonnegative rational `Ω`;
> - `encoding`, exactly one of the literals `"general"` or `"symmetric"`;
> - `log_precision_bits` in the inclusive range 32–4,096;
> - the free variables of the declared encoding; and
> - the maximum-entropy blocks of the declared encoding, with exact counts derived from the instance.
>
> Under `"general"` the free variables are every free distribution and `μ` in canonical node order (§5.2), and there is one maximum-entropy block for every occurrence of `H_D^max(ρ)`. Under `"symmetric"` they are the group array of `0007_spec.md` §3.3 and the group blocks of §3.4.
>
> Each maximum-entropy block contains exact rational `y`, `λ₀`, all `λ_W(w)`, and `ε`. The checker recomputes `ρ`, `D`, marginals, counts, and all derived values; these MUST NOT be trusted from redundant certificate fields.

`encoding` has no default. A payload that omits it MUST be rejected with `missing_field`, and a payload whose `encoding` is any other string MUST be rejected with `schema_mismatch`. A decoder MUST NOT infer the encoding from which other keys are present.

The envelope of §6.1, the rational grammar of §6.2, the canonicalization rules of §6.3, and the limits of §6.4 apply unchanged to both encodings. The `claim` object is unchanged: `q`, `l_star`, and `omega` are where they were.

### 3.2 The group array and its order

Let `Groups(ℓ*)` be the set of level-shape pairs reachable in the canonical tree of §5.2. The decoder MUST enumerate it from the instance and MUST NOT read a declared group count from the certificate.

Enumeration at the supported levels gives `Groups(2)` 15 pairs, `Groups(3)` 60, and `Groups(4)` 213, which equals `Σ_(ℓ=2..ℓ*) |S_ℓ|` in each case: every shape of every level from two to `ℓ*` is reachable. An implementation MAY use that identity to size its storage but MUST still derive membership from the tree, so that a future change to A.1 cannot silently desynchronize the producer and the checker.

`groups` is a positional array over `Groups(ℓ*)` ordered by **decreasing level, then lexicographically by shape**. Decreasing level is the order in which the canonical preorder first meets each group, because the root's children have level `ℓ*`. Shape order is §5.1's.

Nothing in a group entry names its level or shape. As with `nodes` today, position determines identity, and §5.2's rule applies: the decoder recomputes level and shape from position and MUST reject an entry whose variant disagrees with the kind that position implies.

### 3.3 Group payloads

A group entry carries exactly the free variables A.2 gives a node of its kind, with one difference: `alpha` is a single distribution rather than six.

| Kind of `(ℓ,s)` | Keys | Lengths |
|---|---|---|
| positive, `ℓ≥3` | `alpha`, `region_weights` | one entry per element of `Split(s)`, and six |
| zero-shape | `beta` | one entry per element of `C_(ℓ,s_W1)` |
| positive, `ℓ=2` | `mu` | one rational |

The root is not a group. The payload carries it as a separate object `root` with keys `alpha`, one entry per element of `S_ℓ*`, and `region_weights` of length six.

Every distribution MUST be validated against its A.2 domain exactly once, at the group. `mu` MUST lie in `[0,1/2]`. A violation is `bad_simplex` and names the group's `(ℓ,s)`, not a `NodePath`: at `ℓ*=4` a group holds between 6 and 145,800 node positions, and printing one of them would be an arbitrary choice.

The payload object's keys, in the sorted order §6.3 requires, are `encoding`, `groups`, `log_precision_bits`, `max_entropy_blocks`, `root`.

### 3.4 Group blocks

Under `"symmetric"`, `max_entropy_blocks` carries one §7.4 block per occurrence of `H_D^max(ρ)` in the group-level evaluation: one for the root's `α_G` over `S_ℓ*`, and one for each positive group `(ℓ,s)` with `ℓ≥3` over `Split(s)`. Block contents are unchanged.

The order is the root's block first, then, for each level from three to `ℓ*` **ascending**, each positive shape of that level lexicographically. Ascending level matches A20's level-by-level sum of `E_ℓ`, exactly as the general block order does. The group array and the block array therefore run in opposite directions in level; that asymmetry already exists between `nodes` and `max_entropy_blocks` in the general encoding and is not new here.

The block count is `1 + Σ_(ℓ=3..ℓ*) |S_ℓ^+|`, where `|S_ℓ^+| = C(2^ℓ-1, 2)` is the number of positive shapes at level `ℓ`: 21 at level 3 and 105 at level 4. The checker MUST derive that count from the instance and reject a mismatch with `count_mismatch`.

### 3.5 Sizes

| `ℓ*` | General rationals | Groups | Symmetric blocks | Symmetric rationals | Reduction |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 451 | 15 | 1 | 81 | 5.6× |
| 3 | 32,725 | 60 | 22 | 954 | 34.3× |
| 4 | 8,882,281 | 213 | 127 | 26,319 | 337.5× |

Each count includes the claimed `Ω`. The `ℓ*=2` and `ℓ*=3` general figures are confirmed against the committed vectors; the symmetric figures are derived from §3.2–3.4 and confirmed at `ℓ*=2` against the re-encoding in §3.6, which contains exactly 81 rationals.

An estimate of canonical size follows by assuming the 46.84 bytes per rational measured at `ℓ*=3` and reported at `ℓ*=4`, which holds when the producer keeps 48-bit dyadic rounding: `26,319 × 46.84 ≈ 1,232,782` bytes. A symmetric `ℓ*=4` certificate is estimated to be **smaller than the `ℓ*=3` general certificate** already committed at 1,531,818 bytes. This is an estimate from an assumed constant, not a measurement, and Stage A replaces it with the real number.

## 4. The expansion function

For a well-formed symmetric certificate `c`, `expand(c)` is the general certificate defined by:

```text
claim, source_hashes, spec_version, schema        unchanged from c
encoding                                          "general"
log_precision_bits                                unchanged from c

nodes[0]                = { alpha: [c.root.alpha] * 6,
                            region_weights: c.root.region_weights }

nodes[i], i > 0, at level ℓ and shape s of the §5.2 preorder,
    with g = c.groups[groupIndex(ℓ,s)]:
  positive, ℓ>=3        = { alpha: [g.alpha] * 6,
                            region_weights: g.region_weights }
  zero-shape            = { beta: g.beta }
  positive, ℓ=2         = { mu: g.mu }

max_entropy_blocks[j] for the j-th block of the general order,
    attached to node n and region r:
  n is the root         = c.max_entropy_blocks[0]
  n at (ℓ,s)            = c.max_entropy_blocks[blockIndex(ℓ,s)]
```

`groupIndex` is the position of `(ℓ,s)` in the §3.2 order and `blockIndex` the position of `(ℓ,s)` in the §3.4 order. Neither the group order nor the block order is required to align with the preorder; the mapping is by `(ℓ,s)` lookup, and an implementation that relies on an ordering coincidence is wrong even where it happens to agree.

`expand` MUST be total on certificates that pass §3.1–3.4, and its output MUST be canonical under §6.3. `expand` MUST NOT be invoked as a precondition of checking: the whole purpose of the encoding is that at `ℓ*=4` the expansion is never materialized.

The semantic rule is one line, and it is what makes the encoding safe to reason about:

```text
Meaning(c) := Meaning(expand(c))                                 (E1)
```

A symmetric certificate makes no statement about any object other than its own expansion. In particular it makes no statement about a float point, an optimizer run, or a general certificate from which it was derived.

## 5. The proof obligation

This is the substance of the document. Everything above is data layout; everything below decides whether the layout may be trusted.

### 5.1 The theorem

Let `SymCert` be the Lean structure decoded from a symmetric certificate, `SymCert.expand : SymCert → TrackACert` the §4 function, and `SymCert.check : SymCert → Bool` the group-level checker. The following MUST be proved:

```lean
theorem SymCert.check_implies_expand {c : SymCert} (h : c.check = true) :
    (c.expand).check = true

theorem SymCert.omega_le_of_check {c : SymCert} (h : c.check = true) :
    omegaExponent ≤ ((c.omega : ℚ) : ℝ) :=
  TrackACert.omega_le_of_check (SymCert.check_implies_expand h)
```

`TrackACert.check` and `TrackACert.omega_le_of_check` are the existing declarations in `lean/MatrixMath/Certificate/OmegaCheck.lean`. The second theorem is therefore three lines given the first, and `#print axioms` on it MUST list Lean's standard axioms and `AX1_combination_loss` and nothing else.

The implication is the minimum. The equality

```lean
theorem SymCert.check_eq_expand (c : SymCert) : c.check = (c.expand).check
```

SHOULD be proved instead, because it also fixes the differential test of §9.3 in its exact form. An implementation that proves only the implication MUST record in ADR 0010 which direction failed and MUST weaken the differential test to the implication, never to agreement on sampled fixtures alone.

The corresponding byte-level statement mirrors `MatrixMath.Schema.omega_le_of_acceptsOmega`:

```lean
theorem omega_le_of_acceptsSymmetricOmega {limits : Limits} {bytes : ByteArray} {claim : ℚ}
    (h : acceptsSymmetricOmega limits bytes claim = true) :
    omegaExponent ≤ (claim : ℝ)
```

### 5.2 Identity 1 — mass aggregation (A2, A3)

Define the group mass of a symmetric certificate by

```text
M(ℓ*, s) = α_G(s),                                               (S1)

M(ℓ-1, u) = Σ_(s∈S_ℓ : u∈Split(s))
              M(ℓ, s) (α_(ℓ,s)(u) + α_(ℓ,s)(s-u)).                (S2)
```

S1 is A2 summed over the six regions: `Σ_r A_G^(r) α_G^(r)(s) = α_G(s)` because `α_G^(r)` does not depend on `r` and `A_G∈Δ([6])`. S2 is A3 summed over the six regions and over the nodes of group `(ℓ,s)`, using the same two facts. Together with the A6 collapse of §5.4, these are the only places where `Σ_r A^(r) = 1` does any work; §5.4 shows that A15 does not use it.

The Lean-checkable proposition:

```lean
theorem groupMass_eq_sum (c : SymCert) (ℓ : ℕ) (s : AShape) :
    c.groupMass ℓ s
      = (((c.expand).inst.nodes.filter fun p => p.1 = ℓ ∧ (p.2.2).shape = s).map
          fun p => p.2.1).sum
```

using the existing `AInstance.nodes : List (ℕ × ℚ × ANode)` of level, mass, and node.

### 5.3 Identity 2 — the split-complement pairing (A.1, A.5)

`u ↦ s-u` reverses the canonical lexicographic order of `Split(s)`, so the complement of the `m`-th split is the `m`-th from the end. This is what `python/mm_opt/symmetric.py` relies on when it writes `weights + weights.flip(0)` and what `_split_pairs` encodes.

```lean
theorem splitList_reverse (ℓ : ℕ) (s : AShape) :
    (Spec.splitList ℓ s).reverse = (Spec.splitList ℓ s).map (AShape.sub s)
```

The consequence the evaluator uses is that the weight vector A3 and A12 need is the pointwise sum of `α` with its own reverse:

```lean
theorem complementWeights_eq (c : SymCert) (ℓ : ℕ) (s : AShape) (α : List ℚ) :
    ((Spec.splitList ℓ s).map fun u => α.at' u + α.at' (s.sub u))
      = List.zipWith (· + ·) α α.reverse
```

`splitList_reverse` is a statement about the enumeration order of a staircase region under lexicographic order and is proved for all `ℓ` and `s`, not decided per instance. It is verified numerically for every positive shape at levels 3 and 4 by `python/tests/test_optimizer.py::test_the_complement_pairing_is_the_reversed_split_order`, which is evidence and not a proof.

### 5.4 Identity 3 — region independence (A6) and the A15 permutation

Two separate facts, which the brief for this document merges and which must not be merged.

First, A6 collapses. When `β_(T,W)^(r)` does not depend on `r`, the region mixture is that same value, because `A_T∈Δ([6])`:

```lean
theorem betaOf_of_regionUniform {ℓ : ℕ} {T : ANode} {W : Coordinate}
    (hbranch : T.isPosBranch)
    (hA : T.regionWeights.sum = 1)
    (hregion : ∀ r r' : ℕ, r < 6 → r' < 6 →
        Spec.betaRegion ℓ T W r = Spec.betaRegion ℓ T W r') :
    Spec.betaOf ℓ T W = Spec.betaRegion ℓ T W 0
```

`regionWeights` is the `A` field of `ANode.posBranch`, and `hregion` is discharged from the region-independence of `α` that the expansion of §4 guarantees.

Second, A15 does **not** collapse over regions, and `Σ_r A^(r) = 1` does no work there. A14 places `m_T A_T^(r)` inside each of the three quantities and A15 sums the six regional minima with no further weight. What happens under symmetry is different and weaker: the region-`r` quantity assigned to an absolute coordinate depends on `r` only through the permutation `π_r` of §5.1, because the child data it reads is region-independent. Writing `Q_X`, `Q_Y`, and `Q_Z` for the three A14 construction labels evaluated at absolute coordinates:

```text
E_ℓ = Σ_(r=1..6) min {
        Σ_s M(ℓ,s) A_(ℓ,s)^(r) Q_X(ℓ, s, π_r(X)),
        Σ_s M(ℓ,s) A_(ℓ,s)^(r) Q_Y(ℓ, s, π_r(Y), π_r(Z)),
        Σ_s M(ℓ,s) A_(ℓ,s)^(r) Q_Z(ℓ, s, π_r(Z))
      }.                                                          (S3)
```

`Q_Y` takes the ordered pair, because A8 and A12 sum over `u_Y` while selecting on `u_Z`; deriving the companion coordinate from `cY` alone is the defect `docs/experiments/omega-l3.md` records as caught at `ℓ*=3`, and the symmetric evaluator must not reintroduce it.

```lean
theorem eLevel_of_symmetric (c : SymCert) (ℓ : ℕ) :
    (c.expand).inst.eLevel ℓ = c.symELevel ℓ
```

where `symELevel` is S3 evaluated over groups. This is the largest single piece of the proof, because it needs A14's relabelling rule unfolded at all six permutations rather than at one.

### 5.5 Identity 4 — concatenation is an outer product (A5, A.5)

A5 mixes `β_(T[u,r],W) × β_(T[s_T-u,r],W)`, where `×` concatenates support vectors of length `2^(ℓ-2)` and multiplies probabilities. On the lexicographic index set of `{0,1,2}^(2^(ℓ-1))` that is exactly a flattened outer product, because lexicographic order on those vectors is base-3 positional order:

```lean
theorem supportIndex_append (n : ℕ) (L₁ L₂ : SupportVec)
    (h₁ : L₁.length = n) (h₂ : L₂.length = n) :
    supportIndex (L₁ ++ L₂) = supportIndex L₁ * 3 ^ n + supportIndex L₂

theorem denseBeta_betaProduct (ℓ : ℕ) (d₁ d₂ : Spec.Dist SupportVec) :
    denseBeta ℓ (Spec.betaProduct d₁ d₂)
      = outer (denseBeta (ℓ-1) d₁) (denseBeta (ℓ-1) d₂)
```

with `supportIndex L = Σ_p L_p 3^(n-1-p)`. The identity holds for every pair of support vectors at levels 3 and 4 by enumeration, which is again evidence and not a proof. `denseBeta` is the dense representation the symmetric evaluator uses; the general Lean path uses association lists, so this identity is also the bridge between the two representations and MUST be proved rather than assumed by choosing the same representation on both sides.

### 5.6 Identity 5 — leaf aggregation (A16–A20)

A17's `E_2` and A20's `M_total` sum over leaves by distinct `NodePath`. Under symmetry each sum regroups by group and uses the same aggregated masses:

```lean
theorem leafSum_of_symmetric (c : SymCert) (W : Coordinate) :
    (((c.expand).inst.leaves).map fun p => Spec.localSize c.q p.1 p.2.1 p.2.2 W).sum
      = ((Spec.groupList c.levels).map fun g =>
          c.groupMass g.1 g.2 * c.groupLocalSize g.1 g.2 W).sum
```

`Spec.groupList` is the §3.2 enumeration of `Groups(ℓ*)`; a group carries no level or shape field, because §3.2 fixes both by position. The same statement is required for A16's three level-2 quantities before A17 takes their coordinate minimum.

The brief for this document does not list this identity; it is required all the same, because A20's `M_total` is a sum over 1,080,288 zero-shape leaves and 437,400 positive level-2 leaves at `ℓ*=4`, and the symmetric evaluator visits 213 groups instead.

### 5.7 Why exact equality is available at all

Every quantity above is a rational. `hLower x prec` and `hUpper x prec` from §7.3 are functions of the rational argument and the precision, and `log_precision_bits` is a certificate field shared by both paths, so two nodes of one group produce the identical rational bound. Multiplication and addition in `ℚ` are exact, so

```text
Σ_(T∈group) m_T · hLower(x, prec) = ( Σ_(T∈group) m_T ) · hLower(x, prec)
```

is distributivity and not an approximation. Aggregating masses before multiplying by a bound is therefore exact, and the equality of §5.1 is achievable rather than merely an inequality.

The symmetric evaluator MUST NOT introduce a rounding, truncation, or precision-selection step that the general evaluator does not perform, and MUST NOT choose its series length from anything other than the certificate's `log_precision_bits`. Either would replace the equality by a two-sided error term that no amount of testing recovers.

### 5.8 What may not be substituted for the theorem

- A per-certificate equality decided by native evaluation is forbidden as a substitute, and is in any case useless: deciding `c.check = (c.expand).check` for one `c` requires materializing `expand c`, which is the 1.55-million-node object the encoding exists to avoid.
- A project axiom asserting the equality is forbidden by §3.2 and §3.4 of 0001, which permit `AX1_combination_loss`, Lean's standard axioms, and under CN one certificate-specific native-evaluation axiom, and nothing else.
- Agreement of the Rust symmetric evaluator with the Rust general evaluator is a §12.6 differential test. It is required by §9.3 and it is not evidence for the theorem.
- Proving the symmetric evaluator sound directly against A.1–A.10, bypassing `expand`, is permitted but SHOULD NOT be chosen: it duplicates the whole soundness chain of `check_sound` instead of reusing it, and it loses the property in §8 that soundness does not depend on `expand` being the intended replication.

## 6. Rejection

A checker MUST reject rather than accept a weaker claim. Three decidable classes, each with a stable §5.4 error code.

**Structural.** Decided from the instance alone: `groups` whose length differs from `|Groups(ℓ*)|`, or `max_entropy_blocks` whose length differs from `1 + Σ_(ℓ=3..ℓ*) |S_ℓ^+|`, is `count_mismatch`; a group entry whose variant disagrees with the kind its position implies is `bad_path`; an `alpha`, `beta`, `region_weights`, or `λ_W` array whose length differs from the length its `(ℓ,s)` implies is `count_mismatch`; an unknown key is `unknown_field`; a missing or unrecognized `encoding` is `missing_field` or `schema_mismatch` per §3.1.

**Domain.** Decided from the values: a distribution failing nonnegativity or normalization, or `μ` outside `[0,1/2]`, is `bad_simplex`; block failures keep their existing §7.4 codes. Validation happens once per group, and §5 is what makes that equivalent to validating at every node.

**Symmetry of a general input.** A general certificate is *representable* in symmetric form when

```text
Representable(c) :=
    (∀ nodes T, T' with level(T)=level(T') and shape(T)=shape(T'):
        freeVars(T) = freeVars(T'))
  ∧ (∀ node T carrying α, ∀ r,r'∈[6]: α_T^(r) = α_T^(r'))
  ∧ (∀ blocks b, b' attached to nodes of one group: b = b').      (R1)
```

Equality is exact rational equality on decoded values, so R1 is decidable at any `ℓ*` where the general certificate can be decoded. The converter from general to symmetric form MUST decide R1 and MUST reject a certificate that fails it with a new stable error code `symmetry_violated`, naming the first failing group and the two `NodePath` values or the two regions whose values differ.

The converter MUST NOT average, project, round, or select a representative in response to a failure, and `mm certify` MUST NOT emit a symmetric certificate from a point that is not symmetric. `tests/vectors/omega-l2-optimized.json` is the curated negative fixture for this class: it is a valid, CN-certified general certificate whose root `α` is region-dependent and twelve of whose fifteen groups differ across their copies, so it is correct as a general certificate and correctly has no symmetric encoding.

The third clause of R1 is worth stating separately: a general certificate whose free variables are symmetric but whose maximum-entropy blocks differ across a group is valid and has no symmetric encoding. That is not a defect in the certificate; it is a consequence of the block being producer-chosen data rather than a function of `ρ`.

## 7. The general encoding remains normative

- The general encoding remains accepted at every `ℓ*` in §0.2's range. A checker MUST accept it and MUST NOT require the symmetric form.
- The general encoding remains the definition of meaning. E1 defines a symmetric certificate's meaning as its expansion's, so the symmetric form adds an encoding and never a semantics.
- A result published in symmetric form MUST be re-checkable in general form at any `ℓ*` where the expansion fits. The release verifier MUST attempt the expansion under the §6.8 envelope of two hours and 8 GiB and MUST record either the general-form verdict and the expansion's canonical digest, or a `resource_limit` outcome with its measurement.
- As measured, `ℓ*=2` and `ℓ*=3` fit and `ℓ*=4` does not. A `ℓ*≤3` result published in symmetric form without its general-form verdict is a release failure.

§15.2 of 0001 gains two manifest fields: `encoding`, one of the two literals; and `expansion_check`, either the expansion's canonical digest together with its general-form verdict, or the recorded `resource_limit` measurement that explains its absence.

`mm` gains one subcommand and `verify` gains one flag:

```text
mm expand <file> [--out <path>]
mm verify <file> [--profile ck|cn] [--json] [--expand]
```

`mm expand` writes the canonical general certificate of §4 and prints its digest. `mm verify --expand` runs both paths and returns exit code 6 on any disagreement, matching §12.6. §9.2's normative recipe list gains one line, `expand FILE`, which MUST fail rather than succeed silently until Stage A implements it.

## 8. Trust analysis

The checked surface grows. The Lean checker gains a second evaluation path whose structure does not correspond one-to-one with Appendix A's tree: it computes per-group aggregates that no single node of A.1 owns. Stated plainly, the new failure mode is a symmetric evaluator that accepts data the general evaluator would reject, and the wrong `Ω` published on the strength of it.

Three things pay for the enlargement, in decreasing order of weight.

**The equality theorem.** §5.1 makes `SymCert.check` provably no weaker than `TrackACert.check ∘ expand`, unconditionally and for every certificate. After it is proved, the symmetric path cannot accept anything the general path rejects, and the new code is not an independent authority but a proved-equal restatement.

**Soundness does not depend on `expand` being the intended replication.** `TrackACert.check` validates well-formedness itself through `wfInstance`, and `check_sound` concludes feasibility of the instance the certificate carries. So for any total `expand` whatsoever, `SymCert.omega_le_of_check` still yields `ω ≤ Ω`. A wrong `expand` can only cause an intended-good certificate to be rejected, or cause a certificate to certify a feasible point other than the one its author had in mind. It cannot make an unsound bound accepted. The trusted content of §4 is therefore totality, not fidelity.

**Differential agreement where both paths fit.** §12.6 gains a vector class: for every curated omega fixture at `ℓ*=2` and `ℓ*=3` that satisfies R1, the symmetric and general paths MUST produce the same verdict and the same rational `lower(E_total)`, `lower(M_total)`, and `upper(requirement)`. Both fit at those levels, so this is a real comparison and not a smoke test. It is evidence about the implementation, not about the theorem, and §5.8 forbids treating it as the latter.

Under CN the dedicated native-evaluation axiom now asserts the closed result of `SymCert.check` rather than of `TrackACert.check`. That is permitted by §3.4 as exactly one certificate-specific axiom, and the equality theorem is what connects it to the general checker. The TCB ledger of §3.3 is unchanged in content.

What remains unmitigated is proof risk, not soundness risk: if §5.1 is not proved, the symmetric path MUST NOT ship at all. §12 states the fallback.

## 13. Non-goals

This document removes one of two barriers to an `ℓ*=4` result. It does not remove the other, and it does not improve any number.

- **It does not improve the optimizer.** The symmetric parameterization at `ℓ*=4` carries 173,802 free scalars, counted from `symmetric.initial_parameters(4, 1)`. The one recorded run at that size, `data/optimize/omega-l4-32547fbaab943f9b`, reached a float `Ω` of `2.3726980283742183` in 3,000 steps and 5,610.362 s on H64 — worse by `5.4 × 10⁻⁴` than the certified `ℓ*=3` bound of `2.372156220666`. A second run at 600 steps reached `2.377944337702321`. Optimizer convergence at that parameter count is the subject of `docs/specs/0006_spec.md` and is untouched here.
- **It does not by itself produce an `ℓ*=4` result.** Certifying the recorded point would certify a worse bound. What this document buys is that certifying it becomes possible at all, so that the optimizer work has a place to land.
- **It does not change Appendix A.** No equation is amended, no domain is widened, and `AX1_combination_loss` is not touched.
- **It does not widen the certified subspace.** A symmetric certificate can only describe a symmetric point. Whether the symmetric *optimum* equals the unrestricted optimum remains an open question at every level: the `ℓ*=2` measurement in `docs/experiments/symmetry-cost.md` compares what a finite-budget search *finds*, not where the optima lie, and it is underpowered against its own noise floor. What §2's correction settles is only that the claim to have tested it was unsupported. At `ℓ*=2` the published point is asymmetric, and at `ℓ*=3` and above nothing is measured at all.
- **It does not make the symmetric path preferred.** §7 keeps the general encoding normative and requires general-form re-checking wherever the expansion fits.

The encoding buys size, and only size. Every guarantee it keeps, it keeps by proving itself equal to the encoding it replaces.
